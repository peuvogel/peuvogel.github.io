HEART Score: Obstructive versus Non-Obstructive CAD
The HEART score triages chest pain at the front door. This e-poster tests whether that same score, taken at admission, anticipates what the angiogram will show.

Background

The HEART score is widely used for risk stratification in patients with chest pain and suspected acute coronary syndrome (ACS). However, its application in patients with ACS without obstructive coronary artery disease (CAD) on catheterization remains less well defined regarding its ability to differentiate distinct anatomical phenotypes.

Objective

To compare HEART score values between patients hospitalized with ACS and obstructive versus non-obstructive CAD in a tertiary center, and to describe the demographic profile of the groups.

Methods

Prospective cohort study including patients hospitalized with ACS in a tertiary hospital between 02 Sep 2024 and 01 Sep 2026 who underwent coronary angiography. Patients with a final diagnosis other than ACS, including endocarditis, myocarditis and pulmonary thromboembolism, were excluded. Obstructive CAD was defined as stenosis ≥ 50% in at least one major epicardial coronary artery; non-obstructive CAD as atherosclerosis < 50% with absence of plaque instability on intravascular ultrasound, when available. Categorical variables are expressed as absolute values and percentages, continuous variables as mean ± standard deviation. The chi-square test and Student's t-test were used, with statistical significance set at p < 0.05.

Conclusion

In patients with ACS undergoing coronary angiography, HEART score values were similar between those with obstructive and non-obstructive CAD. In this cohort, initial assessment using the HEART score did not differentiate the anatomical CAD phenotypes, supporting the need for complementary approaches for better anatomical characterization.
